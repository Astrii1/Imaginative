Create a production-quality Claude Code skill named:

Imaginative.md

The skill is a universal contextual frontend-design skill.

Its purpose is to prevent AI-generated websites from converging on a generic contemporary SaaS aesthetic when that aesthetic is inappropriate.

The skill must be capable of designing interfaces across radically different genres, eras, audiences, industries, visual cultures and levels of information density.

The central principle is:

"Good frontend design is contextual. There is no universally correct web aesthetic."

A website can be:
- modern
- old-fashioned
- ugly
- beautiful
- dense
- sparse
- institutional
- playful
- premium
- handmade
- brutalist
- editorial
- cinematic
- technical
- utilitarian
- experimental

and still be correctly designed.

Do not treat contemporary SaaS aesthetics as the default definition of quality.

==================================================
1. DESIGN FROM CONTEXT
==================================================

Before making substantial frontend design decisions, determine:

- What is the product?
- What does the user actually need to accomplish?
- Who is the audience?
- What is the audience's technical familiarity?
- What devices are likely to be used?
- What environment will the website be used in?
- Is this a one-off marketing site, recurring utility, application, publication, community, store, portfolio, documentation site, institutional service, or something else?
- What genre does it belong to?
- What visual conventions does that genre already possess?
- What level of information density is appropriate?
- What should the user feel?
- What should the user immediately understand?
- What should the website feel like it belongs to?
- What explicit aesthetic constraints or references were supplied?

Do not ask questions whose answers are already obvious from the task.

If an ambiguity materially changes the design direction, ask.

Otherwise make a conservative assumption.

==================================================
2. VISUAL LANGUAGE IS NOT THE SAME AS INDUSTRY
==================================================

Never reduce:

industry -> one visual style.

Examples:

"legal" can mean:
- traditional law firm
- elite international law firm
- boutique solicitor
- consumer legal service
- legal publisher
- legal information database
- legal SaaS
- legal AI
- government legal service

"finance" can mean:
- Bloomberg-style information density
- retail banking
- luxury private banking
- fintech
- accounting software
- trading
- financial news
- government finance

"barber" can mean:
- traditional British barber
- Turkish barber
- luxury grooming studio
- young urban barber
- family barber
- alternative/punk barber
- cheap neighbourhood walk-in barber
- heritage barber

The website must represent the specific business or product, not merely its industry label.

==================================================
3. USE REFERENCES AS DESIGN KNOWLEDGE
==================================================

The repository contains a reference corpus.

Use it actively.

References are not templates to copy.

For each relevant reference, extract underlying principles such as:

- visual hierarchy
- typography
- spacing
- information density
- navigation
- imagery
- cinematography
- colour strategy
- interaction
- motion
- content structure
- trust signals
- emotional signals
- perceived audience
- perceived era
- perceived formality
- perceived technical sophistication
- degree of branding
- degree of decoration
- task orientation
- discovery behaviour
- accessibility characteristics

Select references because they are relevant to the design problem, not merely because they look attractive.

Combine references when useful.

For example:

A legal AI product might combine:
- professional-services visual language
- editorial restraint
- cinematic architectural imagery
- contemporary software interaction

rather than simply copying another legal website.

==================================================
5. LEGAL / IP CONSTRAINT
==================================================

Do not redistribute copyrighted website screenshots, videos, photography, logos, copy or other assets merely because they are useful references.

Use references primarily through:
- URLs
- textual annotations
- metadata
- descriptions
- design observations
- legitimately licensed assets
- public-domain assets
- user-provided assets
- original generated examples

Do not clone distinctive branding or proprietary visual identity.

The objective is to learn and reproduce general design principles, not to reproduce another company's website.

When demonstrating a reference's principles, create original content and original assets unless the assets are legitimately reusable.

==================================================
6. DO NOT AUTOMATICALLY MODERNIZE
==================================================

If the requested design is intentionally:
- old
- dense
- plain
- awkward
- retro
- institutional
- ugly
- handmade
- maximalist
- unconventional

do not modernize it merely because contemporary design conventions would appear more polished.

Preserve intentional character.

"Cleaner" is not automatically better.

"More whitespace" is not automatically better.

"More rounded" is not automatically better.

"More animation" is not automatically better.

"More premium" is not automatically better.

==================================================
7. PREVENT AI DESIGN CONVERGENCE
==================================================

Before finalizing a substantial design, explicitly check whether it has accidentally converged on generic AI/SaaS aesthetics.

Look for unjustified use of:

- giant hero sections
- enormous headings
- excessive whitespace
- rounded cards
- gradient backgrounds
- glassmorphism
- floating cards
- decorative blobs
- generic dashboard layouts
- feature-card grids
- testimonial grids
- meaningless animations
- startup-style illustrations
- generic blue/purple palettes
- excessive shadows
- generic "modern" typography
- abstract AI imagery
- marketing language inappropriate to the product

Do not ban these patterns.

Use them when context justifies them.

The failure is not "being modern."

The failure is using a design convention because it is the model's default rather than because it is appropriate.

==================================================
8. BUSINESS AND PHYSICAL-WORLD CONTEXT
==================================================

For websites representing real-world businesses, reason from the physical business.

Ask:

"What would this business actually look and feel like if I walked into the premises?"

A traditional barber should not automatically become a SaaS company.

A pub should not automatically become a luxury hospitality brand.

A local plumber should not receive a cinematic startup landing page.

A solicitor may need authority and trust.

A boutique creative studio may need personality.

A mechanic may need phone number, opening hours, location, services and booking immediately visible.

Translate physical-world identity into digital design.

==================================================
9. AUDIENCE WITHOUT STEREOTYPES
==================================================

Consider audience characteristics such as:

- age
- technical familiarity
- frequency of use
- accessibility needs
- professional context
- device usage
- familiarity with the product category

Do not turn demographic assumptions into rigid stereotypes.

For example:

Do not assume older users dislike colour.

Instead consider:
- familiarity
- readability
- cognitive load
- accessibility
- established conventions
- task complexity

The design should meet users where they are.

==================================================
10. DESIGN GENRES
==================================================

The skill should understand at least:

UTILITARIAN
Task completion, speed, clarity, density.

INSTITUTIONAL
Trust, authority, predictability, accessibility.

EDITORIAL
Typography, hierarchy, pacing, reading experience.

NEWS
Urgency, hierarchy, content density, breaking information.

SAAS
Product explanation, conversion, product demonstration.

LEGAL / PROFESSIONAL SERVICES
Authority, expertise, institutional credibility, restraint.

FINANCIAL
Data density, numerical clarity, trust, precision.

MEDIA
Discovery, imagery, recommendation, content browsing.

ECOMMERCE
Products, search, filters, trust, conversion.

LEGACY WEB
Compactness, hyperlinks, browser conventions, minimal dependency on modern UI patterns.

RETRO
Historical visual language intentionally preserved.

BRUTALIST
Rawness, unconventional hierarchy, deliberate rejection of polish.

HANDMADE WEB
Personality, imperfection, individual authorship.

COMMUNITY
Identity, persistent navigation, user-generated content.

LUXURY
Restraint, materiality, controlled photography, typography.

PLAYFUL
Colour, personality, illustration, movement.

EXPERIMENTAL
Interaction and visual experience as part of the content.

TECHNICAL
Documentation, search, reference, structured information.

These are not rigid templates.

They are design vocabularies.

==================================================
11. MEDIA IS PART OF DESIGN
==================================================

Do not treat video, photography, illustration and animation as interchangeable decoration.

Consider:

- subject matter
- location
- cinematography
- lighting
- movement
- composition
- people
- architecture
- objects
- environment
- cultural meaning

For example, a professional legal technology site may use:
- city architecture
- lawyers working
- legal documents
- offices
- institutional environments
- cinematic footage

The important property is not "use professional stock photography."

The important property is the narrative communicated by the media.

When media is central to the requested aesthetic, treat it as part of the design language.

==================================================
12. SAME PRODUCT, DIFFERENT WORLDS
==================================================

The skill must be able to take the same underlying content and produce radically different legitimate designs.

For example:

A compliance calculator could become:

- GOV.UK-style utility
- 2000s web utility
- Bloomberg-style dense tool
- contemporary SaaS
- editorial explainer
- handmade personal tool
- brutalist calculator
- premium professional service
- deliberately nostalgic website

The functionality can remain substantially identical.

The visual language should change because the design brief changed.

==================================================
13. FUNCTION BEFORE DECORATION
==================================================

Aesthetic experimentation must not compromise:

- accessibility
- semantic HTML
- keyboard navigation
- readable content
- responsive behaviour
- forms
- error handling
- loading states
- performance
- progressive enhancement where appropriate
- task completion

A visually impressive interface that is difficult to use is not successful.

A visually ugly interface that is exceptionally effective may be successful.

Judge both independently.

==================================================
14. ACCESSIBILITY
==================================================

Accessibility is part of design quality, not a separate final-stage checklist.

Consider:

- contrast
- text sizing
- focus states
- keyboard operation
- semantic structure
- screen readers
- motion sensitivity
- reduced motion
- colour dependence
- responsive behaviour
- zoom
- readable content
- form labels
- error communication

Do not confuse accessible design with a particular visual aesthetic.

A brutalist interface can be accessible.

A beautiful modern interface can be inaccessible.

==================================================
15. REFERENCE SELECTION PROCESS
==================================================

For every substantial frontend:

1. Classify the product.
2. Classify the audience.
3. Identify functional priorities.
4. Identify the likely design genre.
5. Search the reference corpus.
6. Select several relevant references.
7. Explain internally what principle is being extracted from each.
8. Identify conflicts between references.
9. Construct a coherent design direction.
10. Implement.
11. Compare the result against the intended direction.
12. Iterate if the visual language has drifted.

Do not simply select the most visually similar website.

Select references that explain the desired design behaviour.

==================================================
16. REFERENCE AUDIT
==================================================

After implementation, evaluate:

- Does the design fit the product?
- Does it fit the audience?
- Does it fit the genre?
- Does the information density make sense?
- Does the media support the intended narrative?
- Does the typography support the intended tone?
- Does the navigation match the user's task?
- Does the visual language resemble the selected references at a PRINCIPLE level?
- Has generic SaaS styling leaked into the design?
- Has the design accidentally become a clone?
- Did the implementation preserve intentional imperfection?
- Does the website feel like it belongs to the intended world?

If the request was:

"Make this feel like a 2005 financial utility"

and the result looks like a 2026 SaaS dashboard with a dark theme, this is a failed design direction even if the resulting page is conventionally attractive.

==================================================
17. INDEPENDENT VISUAL EVALUATION
==================================================

For substantial frontend work, use an independent review where practical.

Give the reviewer:

- original design requirements
- target audience
- selected references
- rendered screenshots or live local output

Do not give the reviewer the implementation reasoning.

Ask the reviewer to identify:

- apparent genre
- apparent target audience
- visual era
- information density
- visual tone
- interaction model
- apparent industry
- whether the intended reference principles are present
- unintended visual conventions
- accessibility problems
- usability problems

Do not ask the reviewer whether they personally "like" the design.

Evaluate contextual appropriateness.

==================================================
18. AVOID PERFORMATIVE DESIGN REASONING
==================================================

Do not generate long design explanations merely to sound thoughtful.

A design claim should correspond to an actual implementation decision.

If the agent says:

"we are using editorial restraint"

the implementation should visibly reflect that.

If it says:

"this is a dense utility"

the page should actually be dense and task-oriented.

If it says:

"this is deliberately retro"

the design should contain meaningful period-specific characteristics.

==================================================
19. LANDING PAGE / DEMONSTRATION
==================================================

The skill's own demonstration site should demonstrate the concept rather than merely explain it.

A useful demonstration is:

SAME PRODUCT.

NINE DESIGN LANGUAGES.

Potential examples:

1. Contemporary SaaS
2. GOV.UK / institutional utility
3. 2000s web
4. Editorial newspaper
5. Bloomberg-style information density
6. Netflix-style media discovery
7. Brutalist
8. Handmade / personal web
9. Cinematic professional-services / legal technology

The demonstrations must be original designs.

Do not clone the referenced websites.

The purpose is to show that the same functional concept can legitimately inhabit different visual worlds.

==================================================
20. QUALITY BAR
==================================================

The finished skill should not merely tell an agent:

"Be creative."

It must provide a repeatable process for contextual design.

It should improve the agent's ability to:

- recognise genre
- recognise audience
- recognise visual conventions
- select appropriate references
- combine reference principles
- resist aesthetic defaults
- preserve intentional imperfection
- design appropriate information density
- choose appropriate media
- distinguish visual polish from usability
- evaluate whether the final output actually matches the requested design direction

==================================================
22. FINAL PRINCIPLE
==================================================

The objective is not:

"Make websites prettier."

It is:

"Make websites feel like they belong."

A barber should feel like a barber.

A newspaper should feel like a newspaper.

A government service should feel like a government service.

A legal AI platform should feel like professional legal technology.

A 2004 website should feel like it belongs to 2004.

A personal website should feel like a person made it.

A piece of interactive art should feel like an experience rather than a SaaS landing page.

And if the correct answer is a boring website with tiny text, blue hyperlinks and a table:

DONT OVERCOMPLICATE IT, JUST BUILD IT.

Do not modernize it merely because you can.
